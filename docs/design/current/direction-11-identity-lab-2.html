Tradeoffs
Period v2. Unchanged case: device + meaning + system. Now optically corrected — judge it fresh.
Italic hub. The locked signature (upright ink → italic accent) becomes the mark itself: zot is the campus, hub is the place it tips into. One brand move everywhere — headline, empty states, wordmark. Favicon: an italic z, which is quietly distinctive. Risk: at 19px the lean is subtle; needs the colour to read.
All italic. Energy and forward motion, no colour dependency. Risk: italic wordmarks read “sports/racing” fast, and it borrows nothing from the signature system — it flattens it.
Highlight. The wash chip marks hub like a highlighter — “this is the place.” Strongest small-size survival after the period (the chip stays visible at 19px), and it hands us a crimson favicon for free. Risk: reads “label/beta chip” to some eyes; the chip must never gain a border or it becomes a button.
Weight shift. Quietest: thin zot, heavy hub — searching resolving into found. Zero colour, zero device. Risk: at 19px the contrast almost vanishes; it depends on display sizes it will rarely get.

What to accept about 16px
At 16px no three-letter word is readable — not ours, not anyone’s. What survives is the pattern: dark disc, light cap, pink base. That is the same mechanism Reddit’s and Spotify’s favicons rely on, and it is memorable in a way a lone letter is not — which was the objection to the plain “z”. The 16 tight cut (bigger type, thinner padding) maximises it.
Usage rule
Inline wordmark (topbar, documents): zothub, always one line. Stacked mark (avatar, app icon, favicon, social): the disc. Never stack the wordmark in running text; never put the inline mark in a disc. The italic accent hub is the constant across both.
Recommendation: adopt the stacked disc as the universal mark, with the 16 tight cut for browser tabs. If the 16px pattern-read feels too soft after a day of living with it, the fallback is the same disc with hub alone (italic, pink) — still on-system, still not a lone “z”.

Rose — the proposed middle
Neutrals tinted by the accent’s own hue: ivory-with-a-blush light (very close to the Paper light you liked), and a dark of warm charcoal with a rose undertone — #120D10, not yellow, not blue. The crimson/pink accent sits inside its own temperature, so dark mode stops fighting the brand. This is the only ramp where light and dark feel like the same product.
Graphite — the control
No temperature at all: pure neutral greys. Dark is sober and never clashes; light is a hair less generous than the warm ramps. If Rose reads “too pink” anywhere, Graphite is the safe landing — but it gives the brand nothing back, and the accent has to do all the warmth alone.
Recommendation: Rose. It keeps everything you liked about Paper’s light mode (swap them back and forth — the difference is a blush instead of a yellow), and its dark mode answers both complaints at once: warmer than Cool’s blue-green “cheap” cast, and no yellow anywhere. All four ramps are fully measured (text 4.5:1, boundaries 3:1) in both themes.
